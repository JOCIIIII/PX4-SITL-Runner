# A4VAI X8 for the inhouse SITL

SITL model of the A4VAI X8 (S2000 octocopter: 8 × T-Motor MN5212 KV420, T-Motor 16×5.4 CF props,
Hobbywing Skywalker 50A-6S V2 ESCs, 6S, 10.4 kg). It replaces the X8 approximation of
`JOCIIIII/A4VAI-Custom-PX4-File`; meshes, sensors and plugin wiring still come from there.

| file | role |
|---|---|
| `x8.sdf.jinja` | Gazebo model (PX4 renders it to `x8.sdf`); generated by `make_x8_model.py` |
| `make_x8_model.py` | derives every physical parameter (constants at the top) and writes the template from the Custom repo's `x8/x8.sdf` |
| `patch_motor_model.py` | adds the rotor spin-up reaction torque (`<rotorInertia>`) to PX4's Gazebo motor plugin; `build.sh` runs it before the build |
| `patch_imu_plugin.py` | IMU acceleration from the link velocity over PX4's 4 ms sample interval, saturated at 16 g (`<accelFromVelocity>`, `<accelAverageTime>`, `<accelSaturation>`); `build.sh` runs it too |
| `2000_gazebo-classic_x8` | PX4 SITL airframe: the vehicle's own allocation, `THR_MDL_FAC`, gains and limits |
| `model.config` | model metadata |

`scripts/px4/build.sh` installs these over the Custom repo's X8. Rebuild after a change with
`./scripts/run.sh px4 build`. To regenerate the model:
`python3 scripts/px4/x8/make_x8_model.py ~/Documents/A4VAI-SITL/PX4-Autopilot/A4VAI-Custom-PX4-File/x8/x8.sdf scripts/px4/x8/x8.sdf.jinja`

## X8 mode

Set `SITL_AIRFRAME="x8"` in `envs/gazebo-classic.env` (`iris` is the default). One setting
switches the whole stack:

- PX4 runs airframe 2000; Gazebo spawns `x8`.
- `run.sh` copies the airframe into `ros2.env`, so the ROS2 apps use the X8 configs.
  PathFollowing gets `vehicle_type 2`, i.e. `octo.yaml` (mass 10.4 kg, 28.72 N per rotor).
  SUPER gets `static_realgazebo_x8.yaml`: thrust acceleration up to 20 m/s² (the vehicle's
  maximum is 24.7), flatness mass 10.4 kg and drag 1.14 N·s/m. The body-rate limit stays at the
  iris value of 5 rad/s; 3 rad/s made SUPER's optimisation fail about 7× as often.

PX4 resets its parameters when the airframe number changes, so switching back and forth is clean.
Parameters set from the environment (`PX4_PARAM_*` in `envs/px4.env`) are saved, though, and stay
until the next airframe change. Delete `build/px4_sitl_default/rootfs/parameters*.bson` to drop them.

`octo.yaml` also caps PathFollowing's yaw command at 7.5° ahead of the current heading
(`del_psi_cmd_limit_deg`, 20° for the quad). With the vehicle's `MC_YAW_P` of 6.1, 20° asked
for 122°/s, and the slow-yawing octo kept chasing the heading: yaw-rate setpoint std was
61–127°/s, against 16–19°/s on the iris. 7.5° gives ~46°/s, the vehicle's own auto-mode yaw
rate (`MPC_YAWRAUTO_MAX` 45). The setpoint std dropped to ~25°/s.

The flights below were made before the sensor fix (see Validation): the EKF started 6° tilted
and its altitude was 1–3 m off. They are to be repeated.

The apps fly the X8, but their tuning is the iris's. `super_pf` at 7 m/s (x8_super_c) reached
the goal within 0.7 m with ≥ 0.85 m confirmed clearance, but the flight took a 320 m path for
98 m: 2.4 m/s average, PathFollowing cross-track p90 1.4 m, and SUPER optimisation failing
about 16× per success. Speed and acceleration limits for the 10.4 kg vehicle are still to be
tuned.

`noclimb_ca` on the X8 (x8_nc_b, final model) behaved normally otherwise: yaw-rate setpoint
std 24.5°/s, altitude within −0.14/+0.47 m, goal within 0.7 m. But it went through the crown of
the tree at x ≈ 23 m that the iris runs threaded past: confirmed clearance 0.16 m. That policy
was trained for a 1 m class vehicle (0.6 m crash radius, 0.5–1.2 s velocity response). The X8's
prop tips reach 0.77 m from its centre, so the policy is not validated for this airframe.

## What the logs say

Hover, drag and rate-plant numbers come from `scripts/dev/x8_sysid.py` (it runs on vehicle and
SITL logs alike). The thrust slope is the accelerometer response to the pilot's throttle steps
in the three 2026-01-07 manual flights.

| quantity | value | how |
|---|---|---|
| battery | 6S | ESCs are 6S. The logged 42–47 V is double the real voltage: `BAT1_V_DIV` is misconfigured. Hover current is 59 A = 8 × 7.4 A, which the datasheet gives at the hover thrust and ~22 V. |
| hover PWM | 1515 µs (1479–1533 across flights) | `actuator_outputs` in level hover |
| PWM config | 1100–1900 µs (2026-01-07), 1200–1900 µs (2025-07-31) | params; the same physical hover PWM in both, so the ESC range is fixed |
| thrust slope at hover | 0.036 m/s² per µs (0.034–0.041) | accelerometer response to pilot throttle steps, 3 manual flights |
| ESC throttle range | 1000–2000 µs | the datasheet curve gives 0.034 m/s²/µs at hover with this range |
| horizontal drag | 0.11 1/s (0.09–0.14) | body-frame specific force per body velocity, all flights |
| rate plant | K ≈ 100–115 rad/s² per unit torque (roll/pitch, 1100–1900 scale), lag + delay ≈ 30–35 ms | output-error fit of PX4's rate loop (logged gains) to the logged rate response |

## Model parameters

| | value | was (Custom repo) |
|---|---|---|
| rotor links | 0.005 kg, 1e-6 kg·m² | no `<mass>`, so the SDF default of **1 kg** and 1 kg·m² each: an 18.4 kg vehicle |
| body mass | 10.33 kg (total 10.40 with IMU, GPS and rotors) | 10.4 kg + 8 kg rotors |
| thrust | T = 6.074e-5 ω² (datasheet) | 1.5e-4 ω² |
| motor map | ω = (u + 0.2025)·635.2 rad/s, max 727.3 rad/s (u = 1 ⇔ 1900 µs) | ω = 1000 u, max 885: **117 N per rotor, thrust-to-weight 9.2**, a pure u² curve, saturated above u 0.885 |
| max thrust | 32.1 N per rotor, thrust-to-weight 2.52 | 117 N |
| hover | u 0.519 (≙ 1515 µs), PX4 normalised thrust 0.444 | ≈ 0.33 |
| yaw moment | drag torque 0.0199 m × thrust (datasheet) + rotor spin-up reaction J_r dω/dt, J_r 2.8e-4 kg·m² (prop + motor bell) | 0.05 m, no spin-up reaction |
| rotor drag | 3.12e-4 per rotor (0.11 1/s) | 0.008: about 26× the real drag, 147 N at 5 m/s |
| motor lag | 25 ms up/down | 9.5 / 25 ms |
| spin directions | from the vehicle's `CA_ROTORn_KM` (KM > 0 = CCW) | all mirrored |
| model pose | 0 | yaw 0.485 rad (27.8°) |
| collision | boxes: two landing skids (from `bm.stl`) and the hub; compliant contact (kp 1e5 N/m, kd 1e3 N·s/m) | the `bm.stl` mesh, rigid |
| IMU | on the body link; acceleration = change of its velocity over 4 ms, saturated at 16 g | separate 15 g link, force-based reading |
| accelerometer bias | off (turn-on and random walk) | turn-on σ 0.196 m/s², random walk 0.006 |

The motor map is the datasheet's rpm curve at the voltage where the measured mass hovers at
1515 µs (21.3 V). It is linearised at hover (value and slope), and the PX4 command u maps to
PWM as 1100 + 800 u. `PathFollowing` treats thrust as linear in the normalised command, so
its `octo.yaml` uses 28.72 N per rotor. That value makes 10.4 kg hover at the model's 0.444.

## Validation in SITL

- **Hover.** The hover thrust setpoint is 0.444–0.449 against the model's 0.444. In
  real-vehicle terms the hover motor command is 1514 µs, against the measured 1515 µs
  (x8_step_c, after the sensor fix below).
- **Physics.** Angular acceleration predicted from the 250 Hz motor commands with the model
  above, compared with the acceleration Gazebo produced: roll 0.95, pitch 0.91, yaw 0.97
  (VAF 0.96–0.99). The SITL pipeline adds 16 ms.
- **Same commands as the vehicle.** The 2025-07-31 rate-tuning flight (25–80 s, roll/pitch/yaw
  steps) was replayed in SITL with the gains of that flight (`scripts/ros2/apps/x8_replay.sh`).
  Rate plants were identified from both flights with the same fit. Closed-loop rate-step
  responses with the same controller (SITL side flown before the sensor fix below; the
  post-fix replay x8_replay_d is recorded but not yet re-identified):

  | axis | vehicle: rise / overshoot | SITL: rise / overshoot |
  |---|---|---|
  | roll | 68 ms / 13 % | 60 ms / 21 % |
  | pitch | 70 ms / 15 % | 80 ms / 13 % |

  Yaw needs the rotor spin-up reaction torque. With only the steady drag torque (the stock
  plugin), the vehicle's yaw gains limit-cycled in the replay: yaw-torque setpoint std 14× the
  vehicle's and 37 % of motor commands saturated. When an ESC changes a rotor's speed, the
  motor torque that accelerates the rotor reacts on the frame; at yaw-loop frequencies that is
  ~14× the change of the drag torque. With it (`patch_motor_model.py`, J_r 2.8e-4 kg·m²) the
  replay gives a yaw-torque setpoint std of 0.049 against the vehicle's 0.023, a yaw-rate std
  of 10.1 against 7.5 °/s, and no saturation. An earlier 2.1× drag-torque factor matched the
  integrator gain of a yaw-rate fit but not this behaviour; it is gone.
- **Drag.** The horizontal specific force per body velocity is −0.112 to −0.117 1/s
  (x8_replay_d, x8_step_c) against the design value of −0.11 and the logs' 0.09–0.14.
- **Sensors.** `scripts/dev/x8_imu_check.py` compares the IMU and the EKF with Gazebo's truth.
  The Custom repo's X8 failed it: with the `bm.stl` collision mesh the IMU read a 1.1 m/s²
  forward specific force at rest, so PX4's EKF started 6° tilted, its altitude ran 1–3 m off
  and its vertical velocity 0.2–0.3 m/s off for the whole flight. The IMU link and its joint
  were not the cause (body link, 0.5 kg link and fixed joint all kept it). Box collisions
  removed it; the accelerometer bias terms then still left 0.14 m/s of vertical-velocity error,
  because PX4 does not learn a 0.1–0.2 m/s² bias within a flight. Now: IMU within 0.05 m/s²,
  EKF vertical velocity within 0.01 m/s (the iris's numbers).
- **Touchdown.** Landings bounced up to 0.9 m (x8_sp5_a) because PX4's EKF missed the
  touchdown: Gazebo steps the physics at 1 kHz, the MAVLink plugin forwards every 4th IMU
  sample, and a rigid contact stops the vehicle within one step, so the impulse was usually
  dropped; when it got through it exceeded PX4 SITL's simulated 16 g accelerometer FIFO
  (int16) and wrapped to the wrong sign. The EKF kept "descending", AUTO.LAND added thrust.
  Now the IMU reports the velocity change over each 4 ms PX4 interval, saturates at 16 g like
  the real sensor (PX4 flags clipping), and the skids give (spring-damper contact): a 0.7 m/s
  touchdown stays within ~7 g. Bounce 0.01–0.02 m, landing detected 1.1 s after touchdown
  (x8_sp5_b, x8_sp5_c).

## Not modelled / caveats

- Battery voltage sag: thrust per command is fixed at the 21.3 V operating point. On the
  vehicle, hover PWM rises as the pack drains, and PX4's hover-thrust estimator absorbs it in
  both cases.
- The roll/pitch inertia ratio comes from the measurement (Iyy/Ixx = 1.25). The flights do not
  resolve the roll-vs-pitch difference: one tuning flight shows pitch the more agile axis, the
  other roll.
- The 2026-01-07 flights show a persistent yaw-trim split between the CW and CCW motor groups
  (~100 µs). Props or motor tilt are a likely cause. It is not modelled.
